Ten hay carts couldn't shake this bridge. A drum did. In Grumbleton, the king builds a bridge so strong, ten loaded hay carts cross without a creak. So he throws a parade. The drummer starts. Boom… boom… Two hundred soldiers march on. Left. Right. Left. Right. The bridge… bounces. Then the soldiers bounce. Then the king's crown bounces. The drummer bounces, still perfectly on beat. The captain panics and screams: "Stop marching! Everybody… walk badly!" Two hundred soldiers waddle across like penguins. The bridge goes dead still. Here's why. Every bridge has a hidden rhythm, like a swing. Push it at the exact right moment, again and again, and tiny pushes become giant swings. Two hundred boots in perfect step were hitting that rhythm. That's resonance. It's why soldiers still break step on bridges. And why the drummer now takes the ferry.
